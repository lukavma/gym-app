# Research Gaps

Questions relevant to a strength/hypertrophy training app that the current corpus — the 14 processed papers in `docs/research-notes/` (`EVIDENCE-001`…`031`) plus, since 2026-09-05, the six **provisional** registry items `EVIDENCE-032`…`037` (eight sources not yet through PDF/note intake; see the registry's §13 and "Verification pass 3", and the "Provisional registry sources" table in `docs/research-notes/README.md`) — does **not** answer, or answers only very thinly. GAP-07's narrowing and GAP-11/GAP-12 rest on the provisional items and inherit that status. Each gap is classified by:
- **Status:** Open | Partially resolved | Resolved — added in the papers 11–14 integration pass (2026-08-09) for gaps materially affected by the new papers (GAP-01, GAP-02, GAP-03). A high threshold applies to "Resolved": finding a broad, population-level productive range or a narrow supporting claim does **not** by itself resolve a gap framed around exact, individually-precise values (e.g., finding that 1–2 RIR can match failure for hypertrophy does not resolve the full RIR dose-response curve shape; finding a muscle-specific volume signal does not resolve exact per-muscle set targets). "Partially resolved" is used liberally where new evidence narrows, but does not close, the question.
- **Importance to MVP:** High | Medium | Low — how much this gap would constrain or risk-affect a first-version product if left unresolved.
- **Need more research before architecture:** Yes | No — whether architecture/design work should be blocked on filling this gap, versus proceeding with an explicitly-flagged heuristic/placeholder.

**Architecture-blocking standard (applied/re-applied to GAP-01–03 in this pass):** An unresolved training-science question should only block architecture if the uncertainty prevents the software from being modeled safely or flexibly. Most unresolved questions do **not** meet this bar, because they can be represented through configurable rules, interchangeable strategies, evidence metadata, user-defined presets, future calibration, and uncertainty-aware recommendations — e.g., an architecture can store `targetRIR`, `reportedRIR`, `progressionStrategy`, and `recommendationRule` as first-class, swappable concepts without first knowing the universally optimal RIR value or the one best progression algorithm. This is a distinct question from **"research needed before defining a default recommendation algorithm"** — i.e., before the product ships one specific, evidence-derived numeric rule as its out-of-the-box behavior. A gap can therefore be "No" for architecture-blocking while still being "Yes" for algorithm-default-blocking; both are tracked separately below for the three gaps reassessed this pass.

### GAP-01: Per-muscle-group weekly volume landmarks are not scientifically established in this corpus
- **Question:** How many weekly sets does a given muscle group need (minimum), tolerate best (maximum adaptive), or risk overreaching at (maximum recoverable)?
- **Status:** Partially resolved. `EVIDENCE-028` (Baz-Valle et al. 2022) adds the corpus's first muscle-*specific* dose-response signal — a significant, moderate high-volume advantage for triceps brachii but not for quadriceps or biceps brachii — demonstrating that the volume-hypertrophy relationship is not necessarily uniform across muscles. This narrows the gap (it is no longer true that the corpus has *zero* muscle-specific signal) but does **not** clear the high bar for "resolved": Baz-Valle tests only two coarse bands (12–20 vs. >20 sets/week, with an unbounded upper category), cannot quantitatively compare low volume at all, and never defines or tests anything resembling a maximum-recoverable-volume ceiling for any muscle. Exact per-muscle minimum, maximum-adaptive, or maximum-recoverable numbers remain unestablished.
- **What the corpus provides:** Pelland's volume-hypertrophy dose-response (`EVIDENCE-001`) is a *pooled, cross-muscle-group* relationship (β=0.24%/set, R²marginal=22.3%). Baz-Valle (`EVIDENCE-028`) adds a muscle-specific but coarse, two-band comparison for three muscles only (quadriceps, biceps, triceps), with no low-volume arm and an open-ended high-volume category. No paper in the corpus provides precise muscle-group-specific volume thresholds for a full landmark framework (minimum/adaptive-maximum/maximum-recoverable). The only source of that kind of landmark numbers in the repository is `docs/input/rp-volume-landmarks.md`, which remains explicitly a coaching/framework document, not scientific evidence, per the task's own constraints.
- **Importance to MVP:** High — if the product intends to show per-muscle-group volume targets or landmarks (which the presence of the RP framework document in `docs/input/` suggests may be a product direction under consideration), this is likely to be a central, user-facing feature.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No (revised from Yes, per the architecture-blocking standard above). The uncertainty here does not prevent modeling the software safely or flexibly: the architecture can represent per-muscle volume as a configurable, editable range/target with attached evidence metadata (e.g., "population-level reference range, Low-Moderate confidence" vs. a user-defined or future-calibrated value), rather than needing the one true optimal number in hand before the data model or feature can be designed.
- **Need more research before defining a default recommendation algorithm:** Yes. Shipping a specific out-of-the-box algorithm that auto-calculates or auto-adjusts a precise per-muscle volume landmark (e.g., an automatic MEV/MRV calculator) is not supportable with the current evidence — this is where the unresolved science genuinely blocks a *specific default behavior*, not the underlying architecture.

### GAP-02: The shape and robustness of the proximity-to-failure → hypertrophy dose-response curve is unresolved
- **Question:** Does hypertrophy scale continuously and linearly with proximity to failure across the full RIR range, or is the relationship trivial/fragile and possibly non-linear/plateauing?
- **Status:** Partially resolved. `EVIDENCE-029` (Refalo et al. 2024, an 8-week within-participant RCT) adds a third, methodologically distinct and complementary evidence type — a controlled intervention directly comparing failure to 1–2 RIR — finding no credible hypertrophy difference (pd=48%, pd>TE=3%). This meaningfully strengthens (to Moderate, at the claim level — see `docs/evidence/evidence-registry.md` §5 conflict-note update) the narrower, already-shared conclusion that literal failure is not required for meaningful hypertrophy. It does **not** resolve the broader question this gap is framed around: the Robinson-vs-Refalo(2023) disagreement about the shape/robustness of the *continuous* dose-response relationship across the full RIR range is untouched by a single trial that only compared failure against a 1–2 RIR neighborhood (and did not test, e.g., 3-RIR, 4-RIR, or higher against failure or against each other). The full curve shape remains open.
- **What the corpus provides:** Two directly conflicting analyses — Robinson (`EVIDENCE-012`, preprint, continuous, robust) vs. Refalo 2023 (`EVIDENCE-013`, peer-reviewed, categorical, trivial/fragile) — see the conflict note in `docs/evidence/evidence-registry.md` §5. Both agree failure is not required; neither agrees on how much closer-to-failure actually helps. `EVIDENCE-029` (Refalo 2024 intervention) now adds direct experimental corroboration for the "failure not required" agreement point specifically, without adjudicating the curve-shape disagreement.
- **Importance to MVP:** High if the product intends to give users specific RIR/effort targets or auto-adjust programming based on logged RIR; Medium if RIR is only logged descriptively without prescriptive weight.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No (revised from Yes, per the architecture-blocking standard above). The system does not need to know the universally optimal RIR value before it can be designed to store and reason about `targetRIR`, `reportedRIR`, `progressionStrategy`, and `recommendationRule` as configurable, evidence-tagged, uncertainty-aware concepts. A qualitative "closer to failure trends toward more growth, but failure itself isn't required, and roughly 1–2 RIR appears sufficient" framing is now reasonably well supported (Moderate confidence for that specific band) and can inform architecture-level data modeling immediately.
- **Need more research before defining a default recommendation algorithm:** Yes, specifically before shipping a default that prescribes one precise RIR target as "the" optimal value, or that auto-adjusts programming based on an assumed quantified, continuous dose-response curve across the full RIR range. A default that offers a reasonable *range* (e.g., defaulting toward roughly 0–2 RIR for hypertrophy-focused users, framed as a defensible-but-not-precisely-optimized choice) is more defensible than one asserting a single scientifically-derived optimum.

### GAP-03: No paper directly compares progression schemes (linear load progression, double progression, autoregulated/RIR-based progression) against each other
- **Question:** Which method of deciding when/how to increase load, reps, or sets produces superior outcomes?
- **Status:** Partially resolved. `EVIDENCE-031` (Chaves et al. 2024) is the corpus's first direct, controlled comparison of two progression schemes — load progression vs. repetition progression, both trained to concentric failure — finding no significant difference in either strength or hypertrophy outcomes over 10 weeks in untrained individuals. This directly answers the narrow question "does load progression clearly beat repetition progression, or vice versa, when both are trained to failure in untrained lifters?" (answer: no detectable difference), but does **not** resolve the broader question this gap is framed around: double progression was not tested, autoregulated/RIR-based progression was not tested as a distinct arm, trained/advanced populations were not studied, and only one exercise over 10 weeks was examined.
- **What the corpus provides:** `EVIDENCE-031` (Chaves, direct RCT evidence: load vs. repetition progression, both to failure, statistically equivalent outcomes) plus the pre-existing descriptive pattern (`EVIDENCE-015`) that "prescribe a target RIR and adjust load if not met" is a commonly used approach in the literature, plus two individual, non-independently-verified single-study citations with mixed comparative results (one favoring RIR-autoregulation over fixed %1RM; another favoring velocity-based training over RPE-RIR).
- **Importance to MVP:** High — a progression engine is very likely core to any training app's value proposition.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No (unchanged). If anything, `EVIDENCE-031` reinforces this: the architecture should support multiple progression strategies (load-based, repetition-based, and by extension autoregulated/RIR-based) as interchangeable, configurable strategies rather than assuming one is the sole valid form of overload — exactly the kind of uncertainty that configurable, strategy-based architecture is designed to absorb.
- **Need more research before defining a default recommendation algorithm:** Partially. A default that offers users a choice between evidence-consistent progression strategies (or defaults to a simple, non-autoregulated scheme like fixed-rep-target load progression, with repetition progression as an alternative) is reasonably supported by `EVIDENCE-031` and does not require further research. A default that encodes a *specific automated trigger rule* (e.g., "if final-set RIR ≥ 2, add 2.5kg") is a heuristic invention, not a tested finding — see `docs/evidence/product-evidence-boundaries.md` — and would need either further research or explicit labeling as an unvalidated design choice before shipping as the sole default behavior.

### GAP-04: Long-term (multi-month to multi-year) training adaptation trajectories are barely represented
- **Question:** What does realistic hypertrophy/strength progression look like over 6 months, 1 year, or several years of consistent training, especially as a lifter becomes more advanced?
- **What the corpus provides:** Most dose-response studies in the corpus run 6–14 weeks (Lopez: max 14 weeks; Coleman: 9 weeks; most primary studies underlying Pelland/Robinson/Refalo are similarly short). The one paper addressing longer-horizon expectations (Van Every/Nippard) offers illustrative figures (e.g., Benito et al.'s ~1.53 kg average FFM gain over ~10.4 weeks, declining response over time per Kraemer et al., natural bodybuilder ~2.5–4.0 kg/year over a 5-year career) but explicitly frames these as schematic, not a validated predictive model.
- **Importance to MVP:** High for any feature that sets user expectations, displays progress-relative-to-expected curves, or explains plateaus.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No. Architecture can use the existing illustrative figures with clear framing ("general expectation ranges observed in short-term studies," not a personalized prediction), while treating precise long-term predictive modeling as out of scope until better evidence exists.

### GAP-05: Deload dose-response (duration, frequency, timing, and reduced-volume vs. complete-cessation styles) is essentially untested beyond one protocol
- **Question:** How long should a deload last, how often should one occur, and does a reduced-volume/reduced-load deload (the style most coaches reportedly use) behave differently from complete rest?
- **What the corpus provides:** One RCT (`EVIDENCE-025`) testing exactly one protocol (1 week, complete cessation, midpoint of a 9-week block) in one narrow population. The authors themselves explicitly flag reduced-volume-style deloads as untested and note this is a meaningful difference from common real-world practice.
- **Importance to MVP:** High if the product includes any deload-scheduling or auto-deload feature.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No for a *manual*, user-controlled deload feature (existing evidence is sufficient to support a cautious, clearly-scoped feature per heuristic B6). Yes if the product intends to *automatically prescribe* deload timing/duration/style as an evidence-based algorithm — the current evidence cannot responsibly support that level of automation.

### GAP-06: Optimal inter-set rest interval is not addressed by any paper in the corpus
- **Question:** How does rest duration between sets affect hypertrophy or strength outcomes?
- **What the corpus provides:** Nothing directly — this variable is not investigated by any of the 10 papers.
- **Importance to MVP:** Medium — commonly tracked/prescribed in training apps, but not central to the volume/load/frequency/failure variables this corpus focuses on.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No, but architecture should not present any rest-interval guidance as evidence-based from this corpus; if included, it should be sourced separately or presented as a pure user preference.

### GAP-07: RIR self-estimation accuracy moderators (sex, training experience, exercise/limb type) remain contested, not just under-studied
- **Question:** Does RIR accuracy meaningfully differ by these factors, such that different users' self-reports should be trusted differently?
- **Status:** Partially resolved (2026-09-05; rests on the **provisional** `EVIDENCE-033`, so the narrowing is itself provisional until intake completes). `EVIDENCE-033` (Halperin et al. 2022 meta-analysis, n = 414) finds prediction accuracy **not** clearly moderated by training status (β = −0.006 reps [−0.02, 0.007]) or body region — the pooled version of the "external meta-analysis" this gap previously cited without verification. That narrows the training-experience and limb-type halves of the contest. The sex half is untouched: `EVIDENCE-032`'s sex null concerns repetitions at %1RM, a different quantity, and is itself qualified by older single studies. Not closed.
- **What the corpus provides:** `EVIDENCE-014` documents active disagreement across individual studies on all three moderators; `EVIDENCE-033` (provisional) now adds a pooled null for training status and body region; `EVIDENCE-030` found no session-to-session improvement in experienced lifters.
- **Importance to MVP:** Medium — relevant only if the product plans differentiated trust-weighting of RIR data by user demographic/experience.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No. Treating all self-reported RIR uniformly (per heuristic B8: trust low-RIR reports more than high-RIR reports, regardless of user demographics) is a defensible simplification given the unresolved state of the moderator literature — and `EVIDENCE-033` (provisional) now makes "no experience-based trust-weighting in either direction" the evidence-consistent posture, not merely the cautious one.

### GAP-08: Exercise-selection efficacy (which specific exercises best stimulate which muscles) is essentially outside this corpus's scope
- **Question:** Beyond the general training variables studied (volume, load, frequency, failure, tempo, ROM, periodization), which specific exercises are most effective for a given muscle or goal?
- **What the corpus provides:** Almost nothing generalizable — the only exercise-specific hypertrophy-outcome data are a single 9-week calf foot-position study and a handful of muscle-specific ROM findings (quadriceps, biceps, triceps, gastrocnemius). EMG-based activation studies exist but are explicitly flagged (`EVIDENCE-026`) as not validated hypertrophy predictors.
- **Importance to MVP:** Medium — this is tied to exercise-database design, which the original task scope already excludes from this evidence-extraction phase.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No, since exercise-database design is an explicit non-goal of this phase; flagged here only so a future exercise-database effort knows this corpus won't be sufficient for that purpose and a separate literature base would be needed.

### GAP-09: Sex-specific and age-specific (older adult, youth) dose-response differences are thinly and inconsistently covered
- **Question:** Do the volume/frequency/load/failure dose-response relationships established in this corpus generalize across sex and age groups?
- **What the corpus provides:** Most underlying primary studies across the corpus are male-skewed or male-only (e.g., Bastos: 52% of included studies male-only; Lopez: 67.9% predominantly male; Van Every/Nippard's hormone studies mostly male-only), and most populations are young adults (Lopez: mean age 23.4 years; Bastos: 87% of participants aged 18–64 with only 10% ≥65 and 3% <18).
- **Importance to MVP:** Medium — depends on target user demographics; higher if the product explicitly serves older adults, youth, or wants sex-differentiated programming.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No, but any dose-response claim used in the product should be captioned as most directly applicable to young-to-middle-aged adults, with appropriate caution for other groups, rather than assumed universal.

### GAP-10: Safety/injury-risk data is broad (RT-vs-no-RT) but not broken down by specific training variables
- **Question:** Does training closer to failure, at higher frequency, or with higher volume carry materially different injury risk than more conservative approaches?
- **What the corpus provides:** ACSM's umbrella review found no increased serious adverse-event risk across >38,000 participants for RT generally (with all reported nonfatal cardiac complications occurring during aerobic, not resistance, training in a cardiac-disease cohort) — but this is a general RT-vs-control safety signal, not a variable-specific dose-response for injury risk.
- **Importance to MVP:** Medium — relevant for any safety messaging or auto-scaling logic, though general RT safety data is reassuring for a baseline, non-clinical-population MVP.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No for general safety messaging (existing evidence supports "resistance training is broadly safe" framing); Yes if the product intends any specific claim like "high-frequency training doesn't increase injury risk" — that specific claim is not supported by this corpus.

### GAP-11: `reps + reported RIR` as reps-to-failure inside a 1RM equation is unvalidated, and the accuracy of a post-set retrospective RIR report has never been measured
- **Question:** How accurate is an estimated 1RM computed from a logged set as `load × f(reps + reported RIR)` against a measured 1RM, and how accurate is the *retrospective* RIR a lifter records after finishing a set?
- **Status:** Open (added 2026-09-05). Two negative search results in `docs/reviews/estimated-1rm-evidence-research.md` (`Ø-1`, `Ø-2`): no study substitutes `(reps performed + self-reported RIR)` into any 1RM equation and validates against a measured 1RM, and no study measures a post-set retrospective RIR report — every paradigm is a pre-set prediction or an intra-set call-out. A third negative result (`Ø-3`): repetition-equation accuracy has never been pooled in a systematic review.
- **What the corpus provides:** The two halves separately — RIR accuracy (`EVIDENCE-014`, `EVIDENCE-030`, `EVIDENCE-033` (provisional)) and repetition-equation accuracy (`EVIDENCE-036` (provisional)) — plus the ≈ ±10 % individual error band for estimated maxima generally (`EVIDENCE-034`, `EVIDENCE-035`, both provisional). The composition is not tested anywhere.
- **Importance to MVP:** Low for the MVP itself (no estimate ships in Phases 0–8); High for any copy about the post-MVP estimate stronger than "estimate", and for any automation keyed to it.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No. The estimate is a pure, labelled derivation over immutable set logs (ADR-011); the uncertainty is absorbed by a displayed band, a stated noise constant, and an advisory-only boundary.
- **Need more research before defining a default recommendation algorithm:** Yes, absolutely — for any strategy trigger, auto-applied load, or wording that presents the estimate as strength. The design refuses all three (`docs/architecture/evidence-to-design.md` row 20).

### GAP-12: Translating a training load between repetition schemes, and whether a starting-load suggestion helps, has no direct literature
- **Question:** Does converting a logged performance at one repetition count into a starting load at another improve completion, RIR adherence, failed-set frequency, or session quality versus simply carrying the last load forward?
- **Status:** Open (added 2026-09-05). `Ø-10` and `Ø-6` in `docs/reviews/estimated-1rm-evidence-research.md`: no study tests load translation between repetition schemes, and none tests a starting-load suggestion after a scheme change. The nearest evidence — autoregulated versus percentage-based load prescription — is mixed (one meta-analysis null, one network meta-analysis favourable but without credible pairwise differences on the squat; neither promoted to this registry) and does not test this question.
- **What the corpus provides:** Only arithmetic: cross-formula disagreement about a translated load grows with repetition distance (≤ 1.4 % at one repetition, 7.5–10.8 % at four, 17–19 % at eight), which bounds how far a translation can honestly reach.
- **Importance to MVP:** Low (post-MVP feature); Medium for the post-MVP suggestion itself, whose value can only be judged on the owner's own logged data.
- **Need more research before architecture:** No. The suggestion is advisory, never auto-applied, refuses beyond short repetition distances, and writes nothing (ADR-011).
- **Need more research before defining a default recommendation algorithm:** Yes for any claim of benefit; the design forbids such claims (`docs/architecture/evidence-to-design.md` row 20, "not justified" column). An in-app before/after comparison on the owner's own data is an n-of-1 observation, never evidence.
